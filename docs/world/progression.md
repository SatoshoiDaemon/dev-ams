# World Progression and Difficulty

## Progression Model

World progression should be partially nonlinear.

The player is not expected to follow one rigid sequence of maps.

However, progression is constrained by:

```text
geography
adjacent regions
difficulty
hidden paths
transport
campaign events
environmental hazards
faction state
```

This allows players to reach dangerous areas earlier if they deliberately find a route or survive the journey.

Difficulty should not automatically imply an invisible hard level lock.

Prefer natural barriers such as:

```text
strong enemies
hostile weather
dangerous travel
missing equipment
hidden entrances
transport requirements
campaign conditions
```

over arbitrary messages such as:

```text
You must be level 30 to enter.
```

Level or stat requirements may still exist when they are part of an explicit mechanic.

## Regional Difficulty

Regions may have intended difficulty ranges, but these are guidelines rather than mandatory invisible walls.

A region can communicate danger through:

```text
NPC warnings
enemy reputation
environment
visual/terminal descriptions
previous encounters
quest descriptions
bounty values
```

The player remains free to make poor decisions.

If a player deliberately walks into the Golden Desert or Ice Mountain underprepared, the game does not need to protect them from the consequences.

World progression should respect player agency.
